About Bream Bay Fishing

A free, experimental guide to fishing conditions in Bream Bay and Urquharts Bay, Northland. It shows wind, waves, rain and tide for the next seven days and rates each hour for boat, kayak and shore fishing.

The map shows where the seabed suits snapper in each season, and the seabed itself: depth, how far that depth can be trusted, and what the bottom is made of. Tap the water to read them for a spot.

The snapper map is a habitat index, not a catch forecast. Fine-scale catch records for this bay are not published by anyone, so it is built from what studies say about where snapper live, and it ranks places within the bay; it does not know how many fish there are or whether they will bite. How it is worked out, with the studies it leans on, how it tests against the few real records, and what it cannot tell you.

How the ratings work

Each hour is rated from the forecast wind, gusts and wave height. The worst of the three sets the rating. These limits are sensible defaults for small craft, not safety advice, and you know your own boat and ability better than a website does.

  • Heavy rain lowers the rating one step.
  • For kayaks, a moderate wind blowing off the land lowers it one step.
  • For shore fishing, a strong wind blowing onto the shore lowers it one step.
  • At the harbour entrance, wind or waves running against a strong outgoing tide lower it one step.

The seabed map

Depths are in metres below chart datum at Marsden Point, which is close to the lowest tide. Add the tide height to get the depth at any moment. The map joins several sources, best first, and How sure the depth is shows which one each spot rests on:

    Slope and roughness are shown only where a survey measured the seabed closely enough. Bottom type comes from seabed notes and rock on the charts and from a coastal habitat map drawn at a coarse scale; over most of the open bay that map says only "soft sediment", without telling sand from mud. Zoomed in, depth is drawn on 20 m squares where a survey measured it that finely, and elsewhere between the chart's own soundings and contours; slope, roughness and the snapper map are worked out on 100 m squares. A reef or hole smaller than that can be missed.

    Limits you should know

    • The weather forecast is on a grid of about 9 km. It cannot see local effects around headlands.
    • Wave height is the open-bay value. Sheltered water such as Urquharts Bay is usually calmer, and the harbour entrance can be much rougher on an outgoing tide.
    • The tide curve is drawn between the predicted high and low waters at Marsden Point.
    • Moon phase and barometric pressure are left out on purpose: published tests do not support them.

    Stay safe and legal

    • Not for navigation. Check the MetService marine forecast, wear a lifejacket and tell someone your plans.
    • Whangārei Harbour Marine Reserve, drawn in red on the map, is no-take. No fishing or gathering of any kind. See DOC's page for the legal boundary.
    • Shellfish closures apply on Mair Bank and Marsden Bank, and size and bag limits apply everywhere. Rules change, so check Fisheries New Zealand's rules or the NZ Fishing Rules app before you fish.

    Where the data comes from

    • Wind, rain and waves: Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0), from the ECMWF and Météo-France models.
    • Tides: Toitū Te Whenua LINZ tide predictions for Marsden Point (CC BY 4.0).
    • Depth near the coast: LINZ coastal LiDAR, Whangārei to Bream Bay and Mangawhai to Snells, flown in March 2025 (CC BY 4.0, Toitū Te Whenua LINZ).
    • Depth across the middle of the bay: LINZ Shipping Lane 1 multibeam depth model, surveyed 1999 to 2004 (CC BY 4.0, Toitū Te Whenua LINZ).
    • Depth elsewhere, charted rock and seabed notes: LINZ nautical chart data, soundings and related layers (CC BY 4.0, Toitū Te Whenua LINZ). Not to be used for navigation.
    • Bottom type: Kerr, V.C. 2010, Marine Habitat Map of Northland, Department of Conservation.
    • Marine reserve boundary: Department of Conservation (CC BY 4.0). Indicative only.
    • Map and boat ramps: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), tiles by Protomaps.
    • Snapper records the habitat map is tested against: OBIS, from the Ministry for Primary Industries tag database and NIWA research trawl records (CC BY 4.0), and iNaturalist observations whose observers chose CC0 or CC BY. The studies it is built from are listed on the methods page.
    • Sunrise and sunset are calculated for the middle of the bay.
