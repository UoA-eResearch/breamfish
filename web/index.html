About Bream Bay Fishing

A free, experimental guide to fishing conditions in Bream Bay and Urquharts Bay, Northland. It shows wind, waves, rain and tide for the next seven days and rates each hour for boat, kayak and shore fishing.

It does not yet say where the fish are. A habitat map for snapper, built from seabed and water-temperature data, is the next stage. Fine-scale catch records for this bay are not published by anyone, so that map will be a habitat index and will say how well it tests against what records exist.

How the ratings work

Each hour is rated from the forecast wind, gusts and wave height. The worst of the three sets the rating. These limits are sensible defaults for small craft, not safety advice, and you know your own boat and ability better than a website does.

  • Heavy rain lowers the rating one step.
  • For kayaks, a moderate wind blowing off the land lowers it one step.
  • For shore fishing, a strong wind blowing onto the shore lowers it one step.
  • At the harbour entrance, wind or waves running against a strong outgoing tide lower it one step.

Limits you should know

  • The weather forecast is on a grid of about 9 km. It cannot see local effects around headlands.
  • Wave height is the open-bay value. Sheltered water such as Urquharts Bay is usually calmer, and the harbour entrance can be much rougher on an outgoing tide.
  • The tide curve is drawn between the predicted high and low waters at Marsden Point.
  • Moon phase and barometric pressure are left out on purpose: published tests do not support them.

Stay safe and legal

  • Not for navigation. Check the MetService marine forecast, wear a lifejacket and tell someone your plans.
  • Whangārei Harbour Marine Reserve, drawn in red on the map, is no-take. No fishing or gathering of any kind. See DOC's page for the legal boundary.
  • Shellfish closures apply on Mair Bank and Marsden Bank, and size and bag limits apply everywhere. Rules change, so check Fisheries New Zealand's rules or the NZ Fishing Rules app before you fish.
